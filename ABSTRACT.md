> Abstract

This project develops and evaluates a tone-marked Igbo lexical dataset and investigates the extent to which Adaption Labs preserves explicit tone information during dataset adaptation. The dataset consists of Igbo lexical items paired with their English meanings and associated tone information, while retaining Igbo-specific orthographic characters. The research question guiding the study is: To what extent does Adaption Labs preserve tone information in a tone-marked Igbo lexical dataset?

A prepared dataset of 706 lexical items was converted into an instruction-style prompt–completion format. A four-row pilot was first conducted to verify the dataset structure and adaptation workflow, after which the larger dataset was processed through Adaption Labs. The adapted output was exported and compared with the original lexical forms without overwriting the source dataset. The evaluation distinguished between exact preservation of the original tone-marked lexical form, preservation of the underlying lexical form without exact tone marking, and failure to preserve the original lexical form. 

The exported adaptation contained 703 evaluable rows. Only 21 entries (2.99%) preserved the exact original tone-marked lexical form. A further 280 entries (39.83%) preserved the underlying lexical form but not its exact tone marking, while 402 entries (57.18%) did not retain the original lexical form. Although 585 responses (83.21%) contained at least one tone-marked character somewhere in the response, this was treated as a descriptive measure rather than a tone-preservation score. 

The findings indicate that Adaption Labs did not reliably preserve the explicit tonal representation of the Igbo lexical dataset. The results demonstrate the importance of evaluating linguistic features such as tone separately from general textual quality, particularly in low-resource language adaptation. The study also highlights the need to preserve raw and adapted datasets separately and to use evaluation measures that directly reflect the linguistic phenomenon under investigation.
